A centre without cars

Anna Kowalska

The city council adopted a resolution yesterday changing traffic patterns in the very centre. The changes cover eight streets, and the first signs will go up later this month. Officials argue that pedestrians and cyclists will benefit most from the rebuild, because the pavements will be widened at the expense of parking spaces along the roadway.

What will change

Long Street faces the most change and will become a pedestrian zone along its whole length. Residents and delivery drivers keep vehicle access, but only during the morning hours. Councillors say that after a year they will review whether the scheme worked, and then decide whether to extend it to further streets near the railway station.

Opponents of the change point to shopkeepers, who fear a drop in takings once the street closes to cars. The city promises them compensation in the form of lower rent for the first year, plus new parking spaces in a multi-storey car park two blocks away.
